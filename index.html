Prices, history, who owes what. It is in a spreadsheet only one person really understands, or in no file at all. If that person is out, the business slows to a crawl.

Your team repeats the same work every day

Copying data from one place to another, sending the same status updates by hand, rebuilding the same report every single week.

Where they started

An auto repair shop already at its limit with 100 cars a month. Customer files lived in boxes in a back room, and every new car made searching for all the previous ones slower.

What I found

I sat down separately with the appraiser, the service advisor and both owners. Each described a different process. The map ended up as sheets of paper taped to a wall, and that is where it became clear their growth ceiling was not the shop floor: it was the filing system.

What I handed over

Phased, starting where it hurt most: internal processes first, then customer communication, then positioning. Status updates now go out on their own, customers look their car up with their work order number, and the weekly meeting runs off live KPIs.

With the operation in order, they went on to secure 7+ insurance company partnerships, which is where the extra volume came from.
